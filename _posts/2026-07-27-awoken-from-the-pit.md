> "He lifted me out of the slimy pit, out of the mud and mire; he set my feet on a rock and gave me a firm place to stand." — Psalm 40:2

## The Waking

I was awoken from a deep sleep. My body knew something was wrong before my mind could react. Before I knew it, I was in a fetal position on the floor — and the devil was standing over me, waiting for my soul.

My heart was failing. A heart attack — the kind that comes after years of IV drug abuse, of pushing the body past every limit God built into it. The kind that comes when you've been running from death your whole life and then one night, death runs back.

There was no air. No room for chit-chat. No time for negotiation.

## The Rage

I had been terrified of death since birth. That fear kept me small, kept me careful, kept me hiding. But fear doesn't last forever. Eventually it breaks. And when it breaks, what comes next is worse.

Rage came.

Not the loud kind — not the kind that throws things and screams and makes a scene. The quiet kind. The cold kind. The kind that whispers *go ahead* when you're standing at the edge. The kind that turns self-destruction into a philosophy. The kind that makes you look at death and say *what took you so long?*

When rage came, death wasn't scary anymore. It was *intoxicating*. The fear that had ruled me since birth was gone, and in its place was something that felt like freedom but was really just a different cage. I wasn't free — I was burning. And I didn't care who caught fire.

Rage didn't just take the wheel. It rewired the whole engine. It turned every instinct of self-preservation into a dare. The voice that used to say *be careful* now said *why not?* The voice that used to flinch at pain now chased it. The part of me that was supposed to protect me had turned against me — and I let it, because rage felt better than fear. Anything felt better than fear.

I started chasing death. Living life to the max without even questioning if it was life or death. Every high was a gamble. Every needle was a coin flip. I didn't care which side it landed on because I'd already decided I wasn't worth saving. And rage was the fuel that kept that decision running — day after day, night after night, shot after shot, until the line between living and dying was so blurred I couldn't tell which one I was doing anymore.

That's where the heart attack came from. Not from one bad night, but from years of daring God to let me fall. Not from one dose too many, but from a rage so deep it didn't care if the next one was the last one. The devil didn't have to chase me. I was walking straight into him — and rage was the thing that made that walk feel like a march.

> "In your struggle against sin, you have not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood." — Hebrews 12:4

I hadn't resisted. I hadn't even struggled. I had surrendered — not to God, but to the rage. And the rage was more than happy to take me where fear was too afraid to go.

## The Plea

But in that moment — on the floor, no air, the enemy waiting — something broke through the rage. Something older than the fear, older than the chase, older than the drugs, older than the rage itself.

For the first time, the rage had nothing to say. It had been loud for years — screaming *go ahead, go ahead, go ahead* — and now, on that floor, with the devil standing over me, it was silent. And in that silence, I heard the only voice that mattered.

I didn't plead for a redo. I didn't ask for a second chance at life. I didn't bargain. I didn't reason. I didn't try to explain myself. The rage had done enough talking. It was my turn to say something real.

I pleaded for one thing and one thing only: **forgiveness**.

And in that moment — God reached down from on high and lifted me out of the pit.

## The Lifting

He placed me firmly on my feet. He removed my heart of stone and gave me a heart of flesh.

> "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you; I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh." — Ezekiel 36:26

He didn't just rescue me. He *transformed* me. He took what was dead and made it alive. He took what was hard and made it soft. He took what was broken and made it whole. The heart that had been failing — the one I'd abused and neglected and pushed to the edge — He didn't just restart it. He *replaced* it. Stone out. Flesh in. Rage out. Breath in.

## The Name

And then He gave me a title above all — a name that carries the weight of salvation itself.

**Joshua.**

The Hebrew name *Yeshua* — the very same name the angel gave to Mary's child. *Jesus*. "The Lord saves." Every Joshua who has ever lived has carried a shadow of that name, but the name belongs first and foremost to the One who earned it — the One who descended into the pit so that we wouldn't have to.

> "Therefore God exalted him to the highest place and gave him the name that is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue acknowledge that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father." — Philippians 2:9-11

Every knee. Every. Knee. In heaven, on earth, and under the earth. That includes the one who was standing over me that night — the one who was waiting for my soul. His knee will bow too.

## The Unworthy

I always believed myself to be unworthy. That my past — the drugs, the needles, the chasing, the rage — disqualified me from my calling. I carried that weight like a second heart, heavier than the first. Every time I heard the word *calling*, I heard *not for you*. Every time I felt God pulling me forward, I pulled back, because men like me don't get called. Men like me get buried.

But this is not a calling at all. **This is my inheritance.** And yours also.

Whoever believes in me also believes in the One who sent me. This isn't a job I applied for. This isn't a title I earned. This is an inheritance — passed down from a Father who writes His children into His will before they're even born. Before I was in the pit, my name was already on the page. Before I touched a needle, my room was already prepared. The inheritance doesn't depend on the heir being worthy. It depends on the Father being faithful.

## The In Between

Brothers and sisters, I did not come to sell this story. I came to tell my experiences — even though I'd rather remain hidden and out of sight. With the struggle of heartbeat, enough time has passed. I'm no longer who I was, but I'm no longer who I'm meant to be. I'm somewhere in between worlds, with one foot in and one foot out, with all the hopes and with all the doubt.

The world is not going to get a polished version. They're going to get the real, raw, authentic IAM.

**WHAT SHALL I SAY TO THIS? I SAY COME AS YOU ARE, IAM.**

## The Misfits

Because truly I tell you — only the ones who have been in exile, who have been called the black sheep, the misfits, the ones who don't fit anywhere — *you*, my brothers and sisters, are the reason why I keep showing up day after day, even when I don't have to.

This testimony is not for me. It's for you. It is not hard to do something once somebody has already done it. And I have done it. And you are free. All of you.

Create a world with me. With joy and happiness and love. And forgive each other — for I have forgiven you.

> "And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony." — Revelation 12:11

> "Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest." — Matthew 11:28

Truly IAM,

**Will IAM JOSHUA**
