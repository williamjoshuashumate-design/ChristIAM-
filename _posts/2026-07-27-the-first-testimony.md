> "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." — Genesis 1:1

Every journey has a beginning. This is ours.

ChristIAM- starts here — not with a finished story, but with a first step. A testimony isn't about having it all figured out; it's about the One who is faithful through every chapter, even the ones we haven't written yet.

## Why "ChristIAM-"?

The name carries more than words. It holds a declaration.

"I AM who I AM." That's how God introduced Himself to Moses at the burning bush — not as a name, but as an identity. Eternal. Unchanging. Self-existent. The One who was, and is, and is to come.

Christ — the Anointed One, the Word made flesh, the Savior who entered the story not from a distance but from within it. He didn't send a message from on high. He *came*. He walked dusty roads, knew hunger, felt grief, wept at a tomb, and carried a cross. The Creator stepped into creation and let it break Him — so that it wouldn't break us.

IAM — the name God gave Himself, the name that speaks of presence, of being, of *I am here*. Not *I was* or *I will be*, but *I am*. Present tense. Always. The God who is near, who is current, who is active in this very moment.

The dash — the continuation. The ongoing story. The part that's still being written, day by day, step by step. The dash is us — our lives, our walking, our stumbling and rising again. The dash is the space between what God has done and what He will do, and it's where we live.

Put together: ChristIAM- is both a statement and a question. A declaration that Christ *is* — and an invitation to ask, *who am I in light of that?*

## The First Step

There's something honest about a first step. It doesn't pretend to be more than it is. It doesn't claim to have arrived. It simply says: *I'm moving. I'm walking. I'm trusting that the road leads somewhere.*

Every testimony in Scripture started this way. Abraham left his country without knowing where he was going — no map, no destination, just a promise and the One who made it. Moses stood before a burning bush with nothing but a staff and a stutter, and God still said *go*. Peter stepped out of a boat onto water and started to sink — but he was the only one who stepped. He sank, yes. But he sank *walking toward Jesus*, and that made all the difference.

Faith has never been about having the full picture. It's about trusting the One who does. It's not about seeing the destination — it's about knowing the Guide.

And the first step is always the hardest. The road is unknown. The outcome is unseen. The only thing visible is the next footfall. But that's enough. That's always been enough.

## What This Is

This isn't a platform. It's not a ministry. It's not a brand.

It's a testimony — a living, growing, unfinished account of what God has done, is doing, and will do. A record of grace in the ordinary and the extraordinary. A place where the story continues.

Some entries will be long. Some will be short. Some will be raw. Some will be written in the middle of a storm, when faith feels more like holding on than stepping out. Some will be written in the quiet after, when the storm has passed and the ground is still. All of them will be real.

Because a testimony isn't about polish. It's about honesty. It's about saying *this happened* — and God was in it. Not as a lesson or a moral, but as a witness. A firsthand account. Evidence of grace.

## What Comes Next

More testimonies. More chapters. More of the story as it unfolds.

Not because the story is ours to tell — but because it's ours to *share*. And every testimony, no matter how small, has the power to remind someone else that they're not walking alone. That their first step has been taken before. That their wilderness has been crossed by someone else. That the God who was faithful then is the same God who is faithful now.

> "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever." — Hebrews 13:8

> "And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony." — Revelation 12:11

This is the first word. There will be more.
